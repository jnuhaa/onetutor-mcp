What is cortisol?

Free-text

Cortisol is a steroid hormone produced by your adrenal glands—those small, triangular glands perched on top of your kidneys. It plays a starring role in your body’s response to stress, but its influence goes far beyond that.

What does cortisol do?

Free-text
  • Cortisol surges during stressful situations as part of the “fight-or-flight” response. It helps mobilize energy by increasing blood sugar levels.
  • It influences how the body uses carbohydrates, fats, and proteins for energy.
  • Cortisol can suppress inflammation, which is why synthetic versions (like hydrocortisone) are used to treat inflammatory conditions.
  • It helps regulate blood pressure by controlling salt and water balance.
  • Cortisol levels naturally fluctuate throughout the day—typically peaking in the morning and dipping at night. It can dampen immune responses, which is helpful short-term but problematic if levels stay high.

Counterparts of cortisol:

Approved Free-text
  • Oxytocin: Known as the “love hormone,” oxytocin promotes bonding, trust, and relaxation. It can lower cortisol levels during social interactions, physical touch, or emotional connection.
  • Serotonin: This mood-regulating neurotransmitter helps buffer stress and stabilize emotions. Healthy serotonin levels are associated with lower cortisol and improved resilience.
  • Melatonin: Produced at night, melatonin helps regulate sleep. It indirectly counters cortisol by promoting rest and reducing nighttime stress.
  • Insulin: While not a stress hormone, insulin interacts with cortisol in regulating blood sugar. Cortisol raises glucose; insulin lowers it. Their balance is crucial for metabolic health.
  • Endorphins: These feel-good chemicals released during exercise, laughter, or pleasure can reduce stress perception and help bring cortisol down.

What is cortisol caused by?

Approved Free-text

Cortisol is produced by your adrenal glands, but its release is orchestrated by a sophisticated hormonal relay system called the HPA axis—short for hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis.

Explain the Cushing syndrome.

Free-text

Cushing syndrome is a hormonal disorder caused by prolonged exposure to high levels of cortisol, often from long-term use of corticosteroid medications or, less commonly, from tumors in the adrenal glands or pituitary gland that cause the body to overproduce cortisol. Symptoms include weight gain, a round face (“moon face”), thin skin, easy bruising, and high blood pressure.
